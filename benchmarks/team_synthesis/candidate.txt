When combining member reports, preserve the logical scope of each load-bearing claim:
what was observed versus proposed, the population/denominator, the measured cost
components, and assumptions needed for a calculation or analogy. Keep the qualification
next to the conclusion it limits. A source report can itself be wrong: resolve conflicts
against the supplied evidence or expose the conflict. Multiple roles sharing one input
are not independent corroboration. Keep source identifiers for claims that depend on them.
Continue to make useful calculations, analogies, recommendations and new hypotheses;
label their assumptions and give a discriminating test when appropriate. Do not replace
an answer with generic caution, require consensus for an opinion, or add ritual caveats
to simple supported facts. Preserve scope without copying every detail of every report.
